Reading · Câu 1–10
1. As the climate crisis becomes more serious, millions of people around the world are being displaced. This means they are forced to leave their homes because their living environment is no longer safe or suitable. These people are often called climate migrants or climate refugees. They face many difficulties, not only because they must move, but also because they often receive little legal or social protection. Climate change is making many parts of the world harder to live in, and this problem is expected to grow in the coming decades.

2. Climate change affects people in different ways. Some people are forced to leave their homes suddenly because of natural disasters such as floods, hurricanes, wildfires, or landslides. These events can destroy houses, roads, schools, and hospitals in a very short time. When this happens, families may lose everything they own and have no choice but to move elsewhere. Other people are affected by slower environmental changes. For example, rising sea levels are gradually flooding coastal areas and small islands, while long periods of drought make it difficult to grow food. Even though these changes happen slowly, they can be just as damaging.

3. Low-lying island nations such as Kiribati and Tuvalu in the Pacific Ocean are among the most VULNERABLE places in the world. In these countries, rising sea levels are threatening homes, freshwater supplies, and farmland. Some communities are already planning to move to other countries because their islands may become uninhabitable in the future. Coastal regions in countries like Bangladesh, Vietnam, and the United States are also experiencing frequent flooding. In these areas, storms and high tides damage homes and force people to move inland. In rural farming regions, especially in sub-Saharan Africa, climate change is causing severe droughts. As crops fail and food becomes scarce, families are forced to migrate to cities or other countries in search of work and safety.

4. One of the biggest challenges climate migrants face is the lack of legal protection. International laws, such as the 1951 Refugee Convention, were created to protect people escaping war, violence, or political problems. However, these laws do not include environmental or climate-related reasons for migration. As a result, climate migrants are often not officially recognised as refugees. This means they may not have the right to stay in another country, access healthcare, or receive financial support. Since large-scale climate migration is a relatively new issue, many governments do not yet have clear policies to deal with IT.

5. Despite these challenges, governments and international organisations are beginning to respond. Humanitarian organisations such as the Red Cross and the United Nations High Commissioner for Refugees (UNHCR) provide emergency aid after natural disasters. This support often includes food, clean water, temporary shelter, and medical care. These organisations help people survive in the short term, but emergency aid alone is not enough. Climate migrants often need long-term solutions that allow them to rebuild their lives and plan for the future.

6. One important approach is adaptation. Adaptation means helping communities prepare for and adjust to the effects of climate change so that people can remain in their homes if possible. For example, in flood-prone areas, governments can build stronger river banks, sea walls, and drainage systems. In regions affected by drought, farmers can be supported with new farming techniques and crops that require less water. Education and early warning systems can also help communities respond more quickly to extreme weather events. However, many poorer countries do not have enough money or technology to carry out these projects on a large scale, so international cooperation is essential.

7. Another possible solution is planned resettlement. Some countries have started small programs that allow people affected by climate disasters to move legally to safer areas or other countries. For example, New Zealand has discussed offering special visas to people from Pacific islands affected by rising sea levels. However, these programs are still limited and only help a small number of people. Expanding such programs would require political agreement and strong public support, which can be difficult to achieve.

8. As climate change continues to displace people, there is an urgent need for new legal frameworks to protect climate migrants. Discussions are taking place at the United Nations about recognising climate displacement under international law, but progress has been slow. At the same time, countries must work together to reduce greenhouse gas emissions and limit future climate damage. Supporting vulnerable communities, investing in adaptation, and creating fair migration policies are all important steps. Climate migration is a global issue, and it requires global solutions based on cooperation, responsibility, and shared humanity.
